**How Chaillan opens it:** He has personal relationships with CDAO leadership from his OSD stint. A warm introduction from him to the AI RCC program office is worth months of cold BD. **What you'd pitch:** An LLM router that cuts government inference spend 40-80% while enforcing policy-based model selection (e.g., "never route PII to a commercial model"). That hits CDAO's two primary concerns: cost and security.

### Door 2: DIU Commercial Solutions Opening (CSO)
**What it is:** DIU awards prototype agreements (Other Transactions) in 60-90 days without FAR compliance overhead. 51% transition to production contracts. ~$979M FY2026 budget. Specifically looking for commercial tech that "already works and needs to be adapted for military use."

**How Chaillan opens it:** He was a frequent DIU collaborator during his CSO years. A CSO submission with his name attached (advisor, letter of support) gets reviewed, not filed.

**What you'd pitch:** Theriad as a cost optimization layer that sits in front of any DoD LLM deployment. The router evaluates complexity, routes cheap queries to open models and expensive queries to frontier models only when needed. Measurable cost reduction, no new model required.

### Door 3: SBIR/STTR Fast Track (AF/AFWERX)

**How Chaillan opens it:** His relationships at AFWERX are still warm. He didn't leave on bad terms — he left loudly because he wanted faster progress, which AFWERX represents.

## The Pitch Angle for Theriad

Frame Theriad not as "another AI tool" but as **LLM infrastructure for cost-conscious government operations.** The DoD is spending heavily on AI — Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI all got major DoD contracts in July 2025. The problem is now **inference spend**, not model access. The next wave is cost governance.

Key numbers to lead with:
- 80-90% of enterprise AI compute spend is inference (not training)
- LLM routing achieves 40-85% cost reduction in academic and commercial settings
- DIU's AI RCC is specifically targeting GenAI adoption — cost reduction is a core metric

**Chaillan's own Ask Sage was LLM-agnostic by design** — he already knows this is the right architecture. He is not a skeptic you need to convert. He's a validator you need to recruit.

## How to Approach Him

### What NOT to do
- Cold LinkedIn DM with a product pitch — he gets hundreds
- Lead with "we saw you sold Ask Sage" — sounds mercenary
- Ask for introductions before giving him a reason to care

### What to do
1. **Engage his content first.** He's actively posting on LinkedIn and running "In the Nic of Time." Comment substantively on his posts about AI cost in government. Be visible in his network before reaching out.

2. **Send a warm email via his website (inthenicoftime.us).** Keep it short. Lead with the problem, not the product: "Government teams are burning $X/month on frontier models for queries that a cheap open model handles fine. We're building the routing layer. Given what you built with Ask Sage, we think you'd find this interesting."

3. **Frame the ask as his opinion, not a favor.** Ask if he'd do a 20-minute call to pressure-test the government go-to-market strategy. Not "can you intro us to DIU" — that comes later if there's alignment.

4. **Have a DIU CSO draft or Tradewinds profile ready before the call.** He'll ask if you're already in the system. Being ready shows you're serious.

5. **Offer equity or an advisory role proactively** if the call goes well. He's between things, he understands the defense AI stack cold, and he can open the specific doors you need. A 0.25–0.5% advisory grant is cheap for what his network is worth here.
